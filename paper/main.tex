\begin{abstract}
Quantum algorithms for scientific computing---quantum linear-system solvers, Hamiltonian simulation, and the rapidly growing family of quantum ordinary- and partial-differential-equation (ODE/PDE) solvers---have a structure that mainstream quantum programming languages do not support well.
These algorithms are stated relative to \emph{mathematical access models} (block-encodings, sparse-access oracles, quantum random-access memories) whose physical realizations are deliberately left open; they compose deep hierarchies of subroutines whose adjoint and controlled versions must remain available; and they carry precision parameters and structural promises that must travel with the code that produced them.
We present \pqlang, a Python-embedded language that addresses these needs through three coupled mechanisms: (i) a \emph{pluggable oracle} design in which abstract oracles are declared with one of nine named paradigms, carried symbolically as open modules in a register-level intermediate representation (RIR), and linked to concrete implementations---gate networks, QRAM resources, or alternative data-loading strategies---by a separate binding pass; (ii) \emph{algorithm protocols}, structural interfaces through which algorithm libraries declare, check, and compose their input models without extending the core language or its IR; and (iii) a library of \emph{quantum scientific computing primitives}---block-encoding algebra, qubitization/QSVT with a self-verifying quantum signal processing phase engine, quantum data loading, reversible fixed-point arithmetic, and a compiler from pure Python numerical functions to reversible circuits---out of which six distinct ODE solver families and several nonlinear PDE paths are assembled.
The same open program can be partially bound, re-bound to different implementations, exported to a modular textual IR, executed on native register-level backends---including a new RIR-native interpreter that ships with PySparQ---or checked by a dependency-free reference simulator.
We survey existing quantum programming languages, frameworks, and commercial scientific-computing platforms, derive language requirements from the structure of quantum scientific computing itself, and compare feature and algorithm coverage in detail.
We verify the entire library numerically on real backends: a campaign of 475 experiments in thirteen groups executes every algorithm family through up to four independently implemented paths---among them an RIR-native interpreter now shipped with PySparQ and a \texttt{Circuit.to\_matrix} unitary export contributed to UnifiedQuantum, both created for this campaign---and certifies implementations against truncated-oracle references at register-exact scale, with implementation and method errors reported separately and per-family certification status recorded.
The campaign surfaced two genuine defects (an oblivious-amplification iteration and a Schr\"odingerization momentum sign), both now repaired and pinned by regression tests, which we report openly alongside the certified results.
A compositional resource estimator---enabled by the same structure preservation---compiles programs to a module-preserving Toffoli+Clifford+T+QRAM netlist and reports qubit, Toffoli, synthesis-adjusted $T$, and, as a first-class metric, QRAM query counts, with scaling validated against theoretical expectations across ten algorithm families.
Parameter-scaling studies, the repair of the recorded defects, and the physical layer of resource estimation remain future work.
\end{abstract}
